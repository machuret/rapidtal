EXECUTION AND ORGANISATION
1. Walk me through a campaign task you personally delivered. Listen for their contribution, inputs, checks, and final output.
2. How would you organise a week with three competing deadlines? Look for priorities and dependencies, not simply working longer.
3. What information do you need before drafting a social post? Expect audience, purpose, voice, facts, and approval requirements.
4. How do you prevent an approved asset from being replaced by an old version? Look for naming, access, and a clear source of truth.
5. Tell me about an error you caught before publication. Ask how their checking process changed afterward.

JUDGEMENT AND COMMUNICATION
6. What would you do if the brief contradicted the brand guide? Strong answers identify the conflict and seek a decision.
7. A manager has not approved tomorrow’s email. What happens next? Look for escalation rather than unauthorised sending.
8. How would you explain a missed deadline? Listen for early notice, impact, and a realistic recovery plan.
9. Which tasks would you not attempt without support? Expect specific boundaries rather than claims of universal expertise.
10. A colleague requests a customer export by personal email. How would you respond? Look for permission checks and approved channels.

REPORTING AND COMMERCIAL AWARENESS
11. Which measures would you include in a weekly marketing update? Ask why each measure supports a decision.
12. Traffic increased but enquiries fell. What would you investigate? Look for source mix, measurement, page experience, and lead quality.
13. How would you check whether a spreadsheet total is reliable? Expect source reconciliation and date-range checks.
14. How do you distinguish work completed from results achieved? Listen for separation of outputs and outcomes.
15. What would you report when there is too little data to draw a conclusion? Look for uncertainty and a next measurement step.

CONTENT AND TOOL USE
16. Show how you adapt one message for email and social. Expect audience and format decisions rather than identical copy.
17. How do you check an AI-generated claim? Look for a reliable source and removal of unsupported assertions.
18. What is your process for checking links before publication? Ask about destination, tracking, and mobile behaviour.
19. Which tool have you learned recently, and how did you practise? Look for a concrete task and feedback.
20. When would you ask a designer or specialist for help? Expect quality and complexity thresholds.

WORKING REMOTELY AND IMPROVING THE PROCESS
21. What should a useful end-of-day update contain? Listen for completed work, blockers, and the next priority.
22. How would you handle a task that regularly takes twice the expected time? Expect evidence and a scope discussion.
23. Tell me about feedback you initially disagreed with. Ask how the decision was resolved.
24. What do you need from your manager in the first week? Look for access, examples, priorities, and review time.
25. Which part of this role would need the most learning? Strong answers name the gap and a practical learning plan.

USE A SCORECARD, THEN DISCUSS THE EVIDENCE
Suggested scoring anchors
Rating | Evidence
1 — Not demonstrated | No relevant example or an unresolved fundamental misunderstanding
2 — Developing | Partial answer that would require substantial support
3 — Capable with guidance | A workable approach with clear checks and appropriate escalation
4 — Independent | Specific evidence, sound trade-offs, and reliable quality control
5 — Strong depth | All of the above plus thoughtful improvements and awareness of limits
